# Reader review: "Does Regional Integration Divert Trade from Europe?"

*Notes written as if by a subscriber flipping to this piece in a fresh issue of* The World Economy — *someone who works in gravity trade empirics, knows the standard toolkit (PPML, fixest, Head–Mayer), but has no inside knowledge of this project. Reviewed against `PAPER/Draft/paper_final_draft.docx`, the current file (a light revision beyond `paper_master_draft.md`), plus the underlying scripts in `SCRIPTS/` and outputs in `OUTPUT/`.*

## First impression

The title promises a methods critique dressed as a substantive finding, and it delivers on both counts within the abstract. That's a strength: I know by the end of paragraph one exactly what kind of paper this is — not "here is a new stylized fact about EU–ACP trade" but "here is a widely-used regressor that is quietly broken, demonstrated on a case where everyone would have believed the broken result." That's the kind of paper *The World Economy* readership — people who actually run these regressions — will find useful precisely because it's actionable: check whether your denominator contains your dependent variable.

The core idea (Kuh and Meyer 1955, dusted off for gravity models) is not new in the abstract sense, but I haven't seen it applied this cleanly to a share-form RTA regressor before, and the paper is honest that it isn't claiming novelty of the statistical point, only of showing how large the contamination is in a live, publishable-looking result. That framing is credible and appropriately modest.

## What works

- **The reveal is well-paced.** Section 5.1 hands you the "obviously true" building/stumbling-block estimate (−2.482, robust to everything a referee would ask for) before Section 5.2 pulls the rug out. As a reader I was primed to be skeptical of a paper that *starts* by telling me the punchline is wrong — but by walking through the standard robustness gauntlet first, the paper earns the right to then say "and none of that mattered."
- **Equation (1)–(3) are the paper's real contribution**, and they're stated with unusual clarity for an identity-driven argument. I didn't need to reach for pencil and paper to see where $\beta_I = -\beta_N$ comes from.
- **Table 4 is the paper's best table.** Eight cells, every one rejecting the restriction, t-stats from 2.65 to 9.90 — that's about as clean a "the ratio is not innocuous" demonstration as this kind of paper can offer. The framing sentence ("an equal-and-opposite constraint cannot hold when both coefficients are positive") is exactly the intuition a reader needs and most papers would leave implicit.
- **Figure "Coefficient Stability Across Specifications"** and **"IT Share Coefficient Against EU Trade Dependence"** are doing real argumentative work, not decoration — the second one in particular (coefficient vs. mean EU trade share across three sub-periods) is closer to a "here is the mechanism laid bare" plot than anything in the text tables, and I'd have wanted to see it earlier, maybe pulled into the main results section rather than left as a robustness-section figure.
- **The paper doesn't oversell.** Section 5.3's directional split is reported as an open, unresolved asymmetry rather than papered over, and the conclusion's limitations paragraph (associational not causal, single-REC assignment, AfCFTA on the horizon) reads like someone who has actually thought about what a hostile referee will ask, not a pro forma limitations paragraph.

## Still open

- **§4.3's worked dollar-arithmetic example is in the wrong spot, plus a formatting glitch.** It lands at the end of the *third* (side-matched) paragraph rather than right after the overlapping/disjoint paragraphs it actually illustrates, so a reader hits it two paragraphs too late. It also has a stray `$N$` (literal dollar-sign notation) where the surrounding sentence uses italic 𝑁 for the same variable. Move the sentence up to sit right after the disjoint paragraph, and fix `$N$` → 𝑁.
- **Two footnotes carry an em-dash-parenthetical AI-writing tell** (see `ai_writing_patterns_reference.md`, Part 1 #4) and are still in first-draft form: the `FIX_TIMEVARYING_REC_PAIRS` disclosure in SI §S.2, and the EAC coefficient caveat in §5.4. Worth a rewrite before submission; neither has a content problem, just the rhetorical shape.
- **The "$a_{}$" seen in one row of the extracted Table 4 header** is an artifact of text-extraction on the docx's embedded equation objects, not a defect in the document itself — noted only so it isn't mistaken for a real formatting bug if someone else re-extracts the file the same way.
- **"precisely" is reused for the same claim twice**: the abstract ("a large, precisely estimated negative coefficient, −2.482") and the Conclusion (§6: "−2.482, precisely estimated and unmoved by any of the checks"). A repeated exact collocation is a stronger AI-writing tell than either instance alone. Cut one instance, or replace with the actual SE/CI where it matters.
- **"genuine" is reused in the same vague-hedge role twice**: §5.3 ("carries a genuine residual relationship") and the §6 closing paragraph ("measuring the same arithmetic rather than a genuine trade effect"). Name what's actually being claimed each time instead of reusing the intensifier.
- **A dangling sentence fragment in §4.3**, second (overlapping) pairing paragraph: "Regressing on lnI and lnX instead, holding extra-regional trade fixed, so raising I raises total non-EU trade rather than reallocating it." has no main clause, unlike the grammatical first-pairing paragraph beside it. Fix: "This pairing regresses on ln I and ln X instead, holding extra-regional trade fixed, so raising I raises total non-EU trade rather than reallocating it."
- **Wrong section cross-reference in §6**: "The bias scales with the outside partner's share of the country's total trade, roughly one-fifth on average across country-years in this sample (Section 2)." — that statistic is introduced in the Introduction and restated with sourcing in §4.3, not Section 2 (Institutional Background, which only carries bloc-level EPA/IT-share figures). Fix the citation to §4.3.
- **Mild repetition of the "associational not causal" caveat**: stated in §5.4, then twice more in the Conclusion (§6, two separate paragraphs). The third restatement adds nothing the first two didn't — could fold into one sentence with a forward pointer.

`ai_writing_patterns_reference.md` is a checklist for LLM-tell prose patterns (contrast-reveals, em-dash asides, count-announcement openers, etc.) — worth a pass over the full draft before submission, especially any paragraph touched in the last few revision rounds, since these patterns tend to reappear in newly written material even after an earlier sweep caught them elsewhere.

## Net assessment

This reads like a paper that knows exactly what it is: a clean methodological point (share-form regressors can recycle the dependent variable) demonstrated with unusual rigor on a case study interesting enough to carry it (EU–ACP trade policy). The empirical work is careful — every number spot-checked against the replication tables and scripts matched exactly, the robustness battery is genuinely exhaustive rather than pro forma, and the paper resists the temptation to oversell the directional-asymmetry finding it can't fully resolve. Exposition was the main gap between this draft and a smooth referee process; most of that ground has now been closed (abstract hedge, Table 6 header note, EAC/PIF caveats, the REC-transition timeline table) — what remains above is small and fixable with local rewrites, not new analysis.
