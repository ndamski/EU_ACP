# Reader review: "Does Regional Integration Divert Trade from Europe?"

*Notes written as if by a subscriber flipping to this piece in a fresh issue of* The World Economy — *someone who works in gravity trade empirics, knows the standard toolkit (PPML, fixest, Head–Mayer), but has no inside knowledge of this project. Reviewed against `PAPER/Draft/paper_final_draft.docx`, now the finalized version of the manuscript, plus `PAPER/Draft/supporting_information.docx` and the underlying scripts in `SCRIPTS/` and outputs in `OUTPUT/`.*

## First impression

The title promises a methods critique dressed as a substantive finding, and it delivers on both counts within the abstract. That's a strength: I know by the end of paragraph one exactly what kind of paper this is — not "here is a new stylized fact about EU–ACP trade" but "here is a widely-used regressor that is quietly broken, demonstrated on a case where everyone would have believed the broken result." That's the kind of paper *The World Economy* readership — people who actually run these regressions — will find useful precisely because it's actionable: check whether your denominator contains your dependent variable.

The core idea (Kuh and Meyer 1955, dusted off for gravity models) is not new in the abstract sense, but I haven't seen it applied this cleanly to a share-form RTA regressor before, and the paper is honest that it isn't claiming novelty of the statistical point, only of showing how large the contamination is in a live, publishable-looking result. That framing is credible and appropriately modest.

## What works

- **The reveal is well-paced.** Section 5.1 hands you the "obviously true" building/stumbling-block estimate (−2.287, robust to everything a referee would ask for) before Section 5.2 pulls the rug out. As a reader I was primed to be skeptical of a paper that *starts* by telling me the punchline is wrong — but by walking through the standard robustness gauntlet first, the paper earns the right to then say "and none of that mattered."
- **Equations (1)–(3) are the paper's real contribution**, and they're stated with unusual clarity for an identity-driven argument. I didn't need to reach for pencil and paper to see where $\beta_{\ln I} = -\beta_{\ln N}$ comes from.
- **Table 4 is the paper's best table.** Eight cells, every one rejecting the restriction, t-stats from 2.74 to 9.92 — that's about as clean a "the ratio is not innocuous" demonstration as this kind of paper can offer. The framing sentence (an equal-and-opposite constraint can't hold when both coefficients are positive) is exactly the intuition a reader needs and most papers would leave implicit.
- **The Monte Carlo exercise (SI, Table S.2) is a good addition since the last read.** Simulating a true null and showing the naive coefficient turns up a false, significant negative estimate in 72.5–100% of replications once the EU trade share clears 0.10 does more to convince a skeptical referee than another robustness table would have. It directly answers "how do I know this isn't just noise that happens to run negative."
- **The IHS robustness check (§5.2, SI Table S.3) is the paper being harder on itself than a referee would ask it to be.** The positive intra-REC expansion coefficient doesn't survive retaining zero-trade country-years under an inverse-hyperbolic-sine transform, and the paper says so plainly rather than only reporting the specification that supports the headline story. That's the right call, and it's flagged clearly enough that a reader can't miss it (§5.2, §6, SI §S.5 all say it directly).
- **The paper doesn't oversell.** Section 5.3's directional split is reported as an open, unresolved asymmetry rather than papered over, and the conclusion's limitations paragraph (associational not causal, single-REC assignment, AfCFTA on the horizon) reads like someone who has actually thought about what a hostile referee will ask, not a pro forma limitations paragraph.
- **The prose-quality issues flagged in an earlier pass of this review are now fixed.** The dangling sentence fragment in §4.3's second (disjoint) pairing paragraph now has a main clause; the worked dollar-arithmetic example sits directly after the pairing it illustrates rather than two paragraphs downstream; §6's cross-reference to the EU-share figure now correctly points to §4.3 rather than §2; and the repeated "precisely"/"genuine" collocations across the abstract, §5.3, and the conclusion have been thinned to one instance each.

## Still open

- **Table 6's `dev_EAC` cell had a stray sign in the docx** — it read −3.098*** (SE 0.952) against the surrounding paragraph's "+3.098" and the arithmetic (dev_EAC = coef_EAC − coef_ECOWAS = −0.245 − (−3.342) = +3.098). Checked against the actual replication output (`OUTPUT/tables/table6_rec_interactions.tex`, `dev\_EAC = 3.098$^{***}$`): +3.098 is correct. This markdown sync uses the correct positive value; the docx table cell should be fixed at the source before submission.
- **Table 3's EU-component-fixed coefficient also had a stale value in the docx** — it read −0.294 (SE 0.716), disagreeing with the abstract, Introduction, and Conclusion's consistent "−0.289 (SE 0.661)." Checked against `OUTPUT/tables/table3_mechanical_endogeneity.tex` (`IT Share (EU component fixed) = -0.2889 (0.6612)`): −0.289 (SE 0.661) is correct, and the docx table cell is the stale one — likely an unrefreshed number from an earlier run. Same fix needed for the neighboring "IT Share (ex-EU denom.)" cell: the docx table shows (SE 0.510), but the replication output gives (SE 0.4773) → 0.477. This markdown sync uses the correct values from the replication output for both cells; the docx should be corrected at the source.
- **The Supporting Information's own table count is off.** Both its front matter and its §S.3 header say "four tables reported only in summary," but six are actually listed and reported (Tables 2, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10). Minor, but worth a find-and-fix before submission.
- **The AI-writing sweep is worth one more pass regardless.** `ai_writing_patterns_reference.md` is a checklist for LLM-tell prose patterns (contrast-reveals, em-dash asides, count-announcement openers) and is worth running once more over the finalized text, since the last two editing passes (the Monte Carlo and IHS additions in particular) are newly written material that hasn't been checked against it yet.

## Net assessment

This reads like a paper that knows exactly what it is: a clean methodological point (share-form regressors can recycle the dependent variable) demonstrated with unusual rigor on a case study interesting enough to carry it (EU–ACP trade policy). The empirical work is careful — every number spot-checked against the replication tables and scripts matched exactly except the two internal inconsistencies flagged above, the robustness battery is genuinely exhaustive rather than pro forma, and the paper resists the temptation to oversell the directional-asymmetry finding it can't fully resolve, or the intra-REC expansion result the IHS check can't confirm. Exposition was the main gap between the earlier draft and a smooth referee process, and that ground is now closed; what remains above is small, source-document housekeeping rather than new analysis.
